01. A Distribution of Possibilities: the case

A fictional two-stage token tree illustrates next-token prediction. Supplied facts: First token probabilities: sun 0.55, rain 0.45. Given sun: end 0.4, more 0.6. Given rain: end 0.9, more 0.1.

These are complete toy branches; end paths stop, more paths do not.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

First distribution: sun 0.55, rain 0.45: greedy first choice is sun. → Sun branches: sun→end:0.55×0.4=0.22; sun→more:0.55×0.6=0.33. → Rain branches: rain→end:0.45×0.9=0.405; rain→more:0.45×0.1=0.045. → Compare end paths: rain→end is the more probable of the two complete end paths.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Joint probabilities of the four toy branches

Joint probabilities of the four toy branches. Units: probability. sun→end: 0.22. sun→more: 0.33. rain→end: 0.405. rain→more: 0.045.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: rain→end is the more probable of the two complete end paths. Changed condition: Raise end-given-sun to 0.8 and lower more-given-sun to 0.2.

Expected result: sun→end becomes 0.44, beating unchanged rain→end 0.405. Reason: Changing one conditional distribution changes its path products while the first-token probabilities remain fixed.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Conditional probabilities describe what follows a particular prefix. Multiplying along a path gives its joint probability.

The four branch products sum to 1, but the two end paths sum to 0.625 because more branches have not ended. Greedy decoding selects sun then more; it does not guarantee the most likely complete sequence.

Limit: These word labels and tiny tables are illustrative; path probability is not a measure of real-world truth.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
