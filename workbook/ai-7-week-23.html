Complete task and evidence

  • Power concerns rejection probability under a specified alternative model, test and design. Supplied main simulation counts are sixty-two of one hundred at n = 20 and eighty-three of one hundred at n = 40: 62% and 83% estimated detection frequencies. At three credits per independent unit, per-study costs are sixty and one hundred twenty credits.

    The descriptive difference is twenty-one percentage points for sixty extra credits per study. Separate picture has sixty and eighty detections out of one hundred at the same sample sizes, with unit cost two: 60% and 80%, costs forty and eighty resource units. Keep these scenarios separate. Finite simulation estimates are not exact known power, and no simulations are executed by the arithmetic code.

    The null false-positive rate is unmeasured. Effect size, variability, unit dependence and test assumptions matter; changing them needs new model-specific planning. Per-study cost is different from total hypothetical work over all simulation trials. No actual billing, pupil study or guaranteed performance is supplied.

01. Power and resource tradeoffs: the case

A simulated detection-and-resource trade-off. A fictional simulation repeats a study under the same assumed alternative effect, using two independent-unit sample sizes.

Supplied facts: There are 100 simulation trials per design. At n=20, 60 detect the specified effect; at n=40, 80 detect it. Each independent unit costs 2 resource units. Same test and effect assumptions are used.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Estimate simulated detection: Observed detection fractions are 60/100=60% and 80/100=80%. → Calculate design cost: n=20 costs 40 resource units; n=40 costs 80 units. → Compare the trade-off: Doubling cost adds 20 percentage points of simulated detection under these assumptions.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Stipulated simulation and resource records

Stipulated simulation and resource records. Smaller design: Independent units = 20, Detections = 60, Trials = 100, Cost units = 40. Larger design: Independent units = 40, Detections = 80, Trials = 100, Cost units = 80.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Doubling cost adds 20 percentage points of simulated detection under these assumptions. Changed condition: Change the assumed effect or variability but keep the sample sizes.

Expected result: The detection fractions need new simulation; the resource costs stay 40 and 80. Reason: Power depends on the data-generating assumptions, while this stated unit-cost calculation does not.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Power is the probability of detecting a specified effect under a specified model and decision rule.

Repeated simulation can estimate it; the estimate depends on effect size, variability, design and test assumptions, not sample size alone. Limit: Only 100 trials per design produce noisy estimates.

They are fictional stipulated results, not executed simulation or evidence about pupils.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
