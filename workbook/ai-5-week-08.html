  • A loss assigns a numerical penalty to a prediction compared with a target. For this regression lesson, residual e = prediction - target, and squared error is e squared.

    Squaring makes both positive and negative residuals contribute nonnegative penalties. We use ordinary mean squared error without a one-half factor.

  • The mean squared error, or MSE, is (1 + 1 + 4) / 3 = 2. It summarises these three examples, not every possible future input. If targets use cm, residuals use cm and MSE uses square cm.

Check after the first attempt

After your attempt, compare with the supported explanation: Compare objective A wins under MSE; both MAEs equal 1. For this loss calculation, the signed error is prediction minus target.

State the objective before selecting a model, because these two objectives give different comparisons.
