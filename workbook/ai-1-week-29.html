01. Find a Real Problem: the case

A fictional class loses time finding the correct craft tray. Supplied facts: In eight recorded requests, three selected trays lack a required tool; five match.

The teacher permits a paper finder using fictional requests and an approved tray list, with no pupil names.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Observe problem: Three mismatches are recorded out of eight requests. → Define need: Help users choose a tray containing every requested tool. → Set success test: Compare selected tray against the approved inventory for each new case. → Bound the project: Use fictional tool requests; do not collect identity or purchase equipment.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. From an observed difficulty to a testable goal

From an observed difficulty to a testable goal. 4 of 10 choices mismatch | Some re-selection occurred | Cause is not established. Need a compatible choice | Check time and conditions | User can still decline.

Paper suggestion process | Small permitted prototype | Benefit needs a comparison. Measure fit and coverage | Observe useful outcomes | Do not hide no-result cases

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Use fictional tool requests; do not collect identity or purchase equipment. Changed condition: The approved list contains no tray with every requested tool.

Expected result: The finder should report no suitable tray rather than guess. Reason: Success includes honestly identifying unsatisfied requirements.
