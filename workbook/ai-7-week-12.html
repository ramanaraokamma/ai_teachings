W1 · Explain the current example

Keep this task’s inputs,rule and response together.

Complete task and evidence

  • An uncertainty interval needs a target, sampling model and assumptions. Main exercise: independent normal measurements and known positive population sigma. With mean 10 units, sigma 2 units and n=16, sqrt(n)=4, SE=0.5, margin=1.96×0.5=0.98, and interval [9.02, 10.98]. Rounded critical value 1.96 gives approximately 95% theoretical repeated-sampling coverage.

    This interval concerns the population mean; it is not a range for 95% of individual values or a posterior probability attached to the fixed mean after observation. Unknown sigma requires a justified alternative method, and biased or dependent sampling is not repaired by arithmetic. Separate picture supplies B−A estimate −0.8 seconds and appropriate-method 95% bounds [−1.4, −0.2], entirely negative.

    Changed supplied bounds [−1.4, 0.3] include both signs and zero, not proof of equality. Raw data and fitting details are absent: no SE, symmetric reconstruction or refitting claimed. Neither example establishes every-request speed, causality or practical value. Code validates finite nondegenerate arithmetic, not real sampling assumptions.

An uncertainty interval conveys precision under assumptions. Its endpoints, units, comparison direction and method all matter; the interval is supplied here, not calculated from unavailable raw data.

Limit: An interval does not establish practical value or causality, and invalid sampling assumptions can invalidate its interpretation.
