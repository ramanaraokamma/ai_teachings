01. Categories and Labels: the case

A fictional craft club labels cardboard-order cards ready, review or revise. Supplied facts: Allowed quantity: whole number 1–4. Required colour: red or blue.

If any known field fails, revise; otherwise if a required field is blank, review; otherwise ready. Cards A=(4,red), B=(5,blank), C=(2,blank).

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Read policy: Check known invalid fields before checking missing ones. → Card A: 4 is allowed and red is allowed: ready. → Card B: 5 is outside 1–4: revise, even though colour is missing. → Card C: 2 is valid but colour is blank: review.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Three ordered checks for a calendar card

Three ordered checks for a calendar card. First check known values | Any known field fails its criterion | Revise even if another field is unknown.

Then check missing information | No known failure but a field is blank or unreadable | Review because evidence is incomplete.

Otherwise all checks have passed | Day is 1 through 30 and room is A or B | Ready under this record policy

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: 2 is valid but colour is blank: review. Changed condition: Change B’s quantity from 5 to 4; leave colour blank. Expected result: B becomes review.

Reason: The known failure is removed, but a required field is still missing.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Operational labels specify what a word means for this task. Two labellers using the same priority should agree on these cards. Review describes missing evidence, not a hidden assumption that the card is wrong.

Revise is supported by a known failure. Limit: Agreement between labellers does not prove this policy is suitable for every school or that labels are objective truths.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
