01. Recommendation Scores: the case

A fictional book-activity chooser has a 14-minute limit. Supplied facts: Score=2×interest+practice. A=(interest3,practice2,time14); B=(4,3,15); C=(2,5,12).

Require whole nonnegative feature values; time must be at most the limit. Highest score wins; tie by ID.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Eligibility first: A and C fit time; B takes 15 and is excluded. → Scores: A:2×3+2=8.

C:2×2+5=9. → Choose: C wins at score 9 among eligible activities. → Explain exclusion: B’s score 11 cannot override its failed time requirement.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Time filtering and scores

Time filtering and scores. A: Minutes = 14, Score = 8, Status = eligible. B: Minutes = 15, Score = 11, Status = excluded. C: Minutes = 12, Score = 9, Status = eligible.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: B’s score 11 cannot override its failed time requirement. Changed condition: Change interest weight from 2 to 3, keeping time limit 14. Expected result: A scores 11; C also scores 11; tie-by-ID chooses A.

Reason: B remains too long; changing preferences changes only ranking of A and C.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

A score combines preferences according to explicitly chosen weights; it does not erase hard constraints.

Computing a high score for an excluded item can be useful in an audit, but it must not allow that item back into the candidate set. Store both eligibility reasons and score components for explanations.

Limit: Toy interest scores are supplied inputs, not measured enjoyment or a universal definition of a good activity.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
