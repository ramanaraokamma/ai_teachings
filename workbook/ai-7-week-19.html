Consent is specific, not universal. A fictional study asks adults to submit anonymous drawings for one pattern-recognition investigation.

Practice 1: explain the deciding evidence

Which labelled step matches this detail? Record permitted uses and the approved withdrawal procedure.

1. State the purpose

2. Keep the agreement

3. Respect exclusions

Hint if needed: Read the steps in the pictorial case. Match the supplied detail to the stage that performs or records it.

After your attempt, compare with the supported explanation: Keep the agreement Record permitted uses and the approved withdrawal procedure. Research ethics includes what people agree to, how they can decline and how risks are addressed. A contribution does not grant unlimited reuse.

Practice 2: explain the deciding evidence

Changed-case practice: A new project requests the same drawings for an excluded purpose. Which conclusion is supported by the supplied facts?

1. This one example guarantees correct results for every future input.

2. Do not reuse them under the existing consent; seek the required new authorisation.

3. We can decide the result without examining the changed input or the procedure.

Hint if needed: Use the changed condition and the deciding step in the pictorial trace. The answer must say what follows from those facts.

After your attempt, compare with the supported explanation: Do not reuse them under the existing consent; seek the required new authorisation. The new purpose falls outside the stated agreement.
