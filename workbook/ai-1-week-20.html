01. Recommendations: the case

A fictional classroom break chooser recommends a suitable paper activity. Supplied facts: Must be quiet and take at most 12 minutes. Score 2 for puzzles and 1 for solo.

A puzzle,solo,noisy,8 min; B puzzle,group,quiet,12 min; C drawing,solo,quiet,10 min.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Requirements: Reject A because it is noisy despite a preference score of 3. → Eligible candidates: B and C both satisfy time and quiet requirements. → Preference scores: B scores 2; C scores 1. → Recommend: Choose B; preserve the reasons and any later feedback.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Requirements before preferences

Requirements before preferences. A: Quiet = No, Minutes = 8, Score = 3, Status = excluded. B: Quiet = Yes, Minutes = 12, Score = 2, Status = eligible. C: Quiet = Yes, Minutes = 10, Score = 1, Status = eligible.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Choose B; preserve the reasons and any later feedback. Changed condition: Change the maximum time to 10 minutes. Expected result: Only C remains eligible and is recommended.

Reason: B takes 12 minutes; A is still noisy.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

A hard requirement determines whether an option can be chosen at all. Preferences rank the eligible options. The apparent best preference match, A, is not suitable under the quiet requirement.

If a learner clicks C instead, the event alone does not tell us whether they disliked B, misclicked or wanted variety. Limit: One click is an observed event, not proof of satisfaction or a stable personal preference.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.

06. Make the idea visible in your project

Separate sorter requirements, preferences and observed feedback; document why a suggestion was eligible. Draw your input, deciding step, result and limit.

Supported question: Why does the recommendation change without changing anyone’s preferences?

Draw your project trace and justify the deciding step using evidence.
