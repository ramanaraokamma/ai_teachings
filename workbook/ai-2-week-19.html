01. Sampling and Representation: the case

A fictional Grade 7 class wants to understand book-format preferences across its whole grade. Supplied facts: Population: all 60 Grade 7 learners. Frame: current school-approved list of those 60, used only by the teacher.

Teacher randomly invites 15; 12 answer the same neutral comic/prose/either question. No names enter the analysis table.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Define target: The question concerns all Grade 7, not only library-club members. → Select invitations: Teacher uses the complete approved list and documented random selection. → Record responses: 12 replies from 15 invited; three nonresponses remain visible. → Report scope: Invitation response rate 12/15=80%; preference counts describe respondents.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Follow the evidence

Follow the evidence. Target | All Grade 7 students | Population. Available data | Three club responses | Sample. Observed result | 2 of 3 choose Code | Describes respondents

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Invitation response rate 12/15=80%; preference counts describe respondents. Changed condition: Invite only the 15 library-club members instead.

Expected result: The invitation process no longer covers the whole grade through this frame. Reason: Club members may differ in reading interests; selection is now limited to one group.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

A sample plan states who could be selected, how selection happens and what happens after invitation. Random invitations do not guarantee that respondents resemble those who declined.

Use a neutral question and preserve nonresponse coverage. Learners can discuss aggregate counts without receiving the identifiable selection frame.

Limit: Even the random-invitation plan does not remove nonresponse bias or justify revealing identifiable school lists.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
