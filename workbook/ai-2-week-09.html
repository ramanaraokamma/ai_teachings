01. Features and Distractors: the case

A fictional book-topic classifier sees mystery books with triangular logos and science books with circular logos. Supplied facts: Training contains no mystery-circle or science-triangle covers.

Goal is to identify topic from actual subject information, not publisher logo. New controlled test outputs have not been supplied.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Spot confounding: Topic and logo always move together in the training set. → Hold topic fixed: Compare the same mystery description with triangle versus circle logo. → Hold logo fixed: Compare mystery versus science descriptions with identical triangle logo. → Observe separately: Record predictions before concluding which clue the classifier uses.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. The same cards under two feature choices

The same cards under two feature choices. A is 10 by 7 cm and fits | Keep length only | 10 cm. B is 10 by 9 cm and is too large | Keep length only | 10 cm. A is 10 by 7 cm and fits | Keep length and width | 10 cm and 7 cm.

B is 10 by 9 cm and is too large | Keep length and width | 10 cm and 9 cm

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Record predictions before concluding which clue the classifier uses. Changed condition: The first controlled pair yields mystery for triangle and science for circle.

Expected result: That pair is evidence of unwanted logo sensitivity. Reason: The subject description stayed mystery; only the irrelevant logo changed.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Either subject information or logo could explain success on the old combinations. Controlled pairs separate their predictions by changing one aspect at a time.

Designing the pair is not equivalent to observing the model’s response. Keep the descriptions identical when testing logo so differences do not have two possible causes.

Limit: Controlled observations support specific sensitivity claims; they do not reveal every internal feature a learned model uses.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
