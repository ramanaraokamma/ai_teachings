01. Test Edge Cases and Fairness: the case

A fictional craft-book chooser accepts requests for whole available minutes from 0 to 20. Supplied facts: Booklet A takes 6 minutes; B takes 12. Return first duration at most available time.

Check 5,6,7; also 0,20,21 and blank. Same catalogue applies to every user.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Near a boundary: At 5: none.

At 6 and 7: A. → Valid extremes: 0: none. 20: A, because first-fit order remains fixed. → Invalid inputs: 21 and blank require clarification, not ordinary search. → Coverage record: Record each user condition tested; do not invent unrun outcomes.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Three requests around ten minutes

Three requests around ten minutes. 9 minutes | 10 is too long | No match in catalogue B. 10 minutes | 10 fits exactly | Suggest B1. 11 minutes | 10 fits with time left | Suggest B1

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Record each user condition tested; do not invent unrun outcomes. Changed condition: Faulty code uses duration strictly below available time. Expected result: Request 6 now wrongly returns none.

Reason: A’s duration equals the available time and should be eligible under at most.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Adjacent values below, at and above 6 expose an accidental strict inequality. Zero is valid even though no activity fits.

The top valid input does not require selecting the longest activity: catalogue order still controls selection. Coverage also asks whose conditions were tested, which cannot be inferred from these numbers alone.

Limit: Equal written rules do not prove equally good outcomes for every group; actual condition-specific evidence is still needed.
