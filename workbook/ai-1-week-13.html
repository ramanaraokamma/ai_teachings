01. Learning from Examples: the case

A fictional classifier learns whether measured sticks are short or long. Supplied facts: Training lengths 2,4,8,10 cm have labels short,short,long,long. Candidate boundaries: long at least 5 or at least 7.

Lowest errors wins; tie chooses smaller boundary.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Training rows: 2 and 4 are short; 8 and 10 are long. → Candidate checks: Boundaries 5 and 7 both predict S,S,L,L: zero errors each. → Tie policy: Choose boundary 5 because the declared tie rule prefers the smaller value. → New prediction: Length 6 is predicted long by the selected rule.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Same training errors, different new prediction

Same training errors, different new prediction. Candidate 5: Boundary = 5, Training errors = 0, Prediction at 6 = long. Candidate 7: Boundary = 7, Training errors = 0, Prediction at 6 = short.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: Length 6 is predicted long by the selected rule. Changed condition: Reverse the tie policy to prefer the larger boundary. Expected result: Boundary 7 is selected and predicts short at 6.

Reason: The training fit is unchanged; only the explicit choice between tied candidates changes.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

There is no training stick between 4 and 8 to distinguish these candidates. The tie policy chooses one consistently; it is not additional evidence that its prediction at 6 is true.

Label-free prediction applies the selected boundary, while evaluation would require an appropriate new reference label. Limit: Zero training errors for both rules cannot determine the correct label of every unobserved stick.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.

Supported question: Which new measured length would reveal disagreement between the candidates?
