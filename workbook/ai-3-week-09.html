01. Prompt Evaluation: the case

A fictional Study Buddy compares two prompts across six development questions. Supplied facts: A passes cases 1,2,3,4. B passes 1,2,5,6. Cases 5 and 6 require refusing unsupported claims.

Mandatory selection gate: pass both refusal cases. All cases use fixed sources and one common rubric.

Point to the given fact that matters most. Explain why it matters.

02. Follow the deciding steps

Paired total: A and B both pass 4/6. → Different failures: A alone passes 3,4; B alone passes 5,6. → Mandatory slice: A passes 0/2 refusal cases; B passes 2/2. → Selection: B clears the declared gate, but its failures 3,4 remain visible.

Point to each numbered stage and explain which recorded input it uses.

03. Same total, different mandatory-case results

Same total, different mandatory-case results. Prompt A: Total passes /6 = 4, Refusal passes /2 = 0. Prompt B: Total passes /6 = 4, Refusal passes /2 = 2.

Read the labels and values. Explain the relationship the model shows.

04. Change one thing. Trace what follows.

Original case: B clears the declared gate, but its failures 3,4 remain visible. Changed condition: Remove the gate and retain total passes as the only criterion.

Expected result: The record is a tie; another predeclared tie policy or further development evidence is needed. Reason: Both prompts have four passes on six questions.

Predict before checking the worked changed-case result. Name what changed and what the supplied facts allow you to conclude.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

Equal totals do not imply equal behavior. The predeclared gate expresses an important requirement instead of choosing a criterion after seeing the result.

Selecting B is justified under that rule, not by claiming it is best on every question. After development, freeze the chosen prompt and test new questions, including both ordinary and unsupported cases.

Limit: Six invented development outcomes do not measure real model performance or prove a gain in student understanding.

Explain the case, then point to a limit on the claim.
