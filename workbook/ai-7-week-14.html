A small exact paired sign-flip test. Compare three independent task pairs under a null that permits each difference’s sign to flip independently with equal probability.

Practice 1: explain the deciding evidence

Which labelled step matches this detail? Only -4 and 4 have absolute sum at least 4: exact two-sided p=2/8=0.25.

1. Enumerate null sign flips

2. Count equally extreme sums

3. Preserve paired differences

Hint if needed: Read the steps in the pictorial case. Match the supplied detail to the stage that performs or records it.

After your attempt, compare with the supported explanation: Count equally extreme sums Only -4 and 4 have absolute sum at least 4: exact two-sided p=2/8=0.25. A test compares an observed statistic with outcomes under a specified null model. Here the sign-flip assumptions make eight arrangements equally likely. At a predeclared 0.05 threshold, p=0.25 does not reject the null; it does not prove no difference.

Practice 2: explain the deciding evidence

Changed-case practice: Break the task pairing and use unrelated A and B measurements. Which conclusion is supported by the supplied facts?

1. We can decide the result without examining the changed input or the procedure.

2. This paired sign-flip calculation is no longer justified by the stated design.

3. This one example guarantees correct results for every future input.
