Main lesson · Section 2

Understand the question and evidence contract

Show the aggregate result, failure evidence and honest unknowns rather than only a best demonstration.

Start with the research problem

A presentation should help the audience inspect the research, not merely admire an example. Start with the question and design, show the primary counts, then a failure, a limitation and a specific next question. When challenged about an untested device or condition, state the boundary and propose a test.

A confident promise is not a substitute for the missing observations, and a proposed study must remain labelled not run.

Original concept explanation

A presentation should make the evidence easy to challenge. Show the baseline, scope, negative cases and uncertainties instead of only a best example.

    Main lesson · Section 7

    Find the first unsupported inference and repair it

    Use the supplied conditions rather than inventing missing evidence.

    Original qualification and repair setup

    A confident response should not replace missing evidence with speculation.

    A useful check asks what would make the reasoning fail. Distinguish a failure to execute the declared procedure from a weakness in the procedure itself.

    The first may require a correction; the second requires an explicitly revised method and new evidence. Preserve unsuccessful cases so an improved result cannot hide the original problem.
