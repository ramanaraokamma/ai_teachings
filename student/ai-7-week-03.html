Main lesson · Section 2

Distinguish source type, identity and inspected evidence

A citation should let another reader locate the actual supporting statement.

Begin with a newsletter and conflicting original counts

Imagine a school newsletter says a study used one hundred cases, but its original methods describe forty. A later revised record says sixty. Do not pick the biggest number or combine them into a new count. Identify which document supports the claim and record its version and location. Primary research reports the study’s own work; it still needs scrutiny.

If the original is unavailable, label the summary as a secondary, unverified account rather than pretending to have inspected it.

Define primary research and summary discovery

Primary research presents a study's own methods and results. A summary can help discovery but should not replace inspection of the original evidence.

    Main lesson · Section 7

    Audit document identity, revision scope and access status

    Keep uncertainty about missing material in the source record.

    Core source-tracing qualification and failure analysis

    Being primary does not guarantee soundness; methods and evidence still require assessment.

    A useful check asks what would make the reasoning fail. Distinguish a failure to execute the declared procedure from a weakness in the procedure itself.

    The first may require a correction; the second requires an explicitly revised method and new evidence. Preserve unsuccessful cases so an improved result cannot hide the original problem.
