Main lesson · Section 2

A red bus display: what do we really know?

First describe what you can see. Then look for evidence about what happened inside.

Meet Leila and Arun

The school bus display says DELAY 12 MINUTES in red. Leila calls it AI because it predicted the delay. Arun calls it a simple rule because the number turned red.

Both have noticed a real part of the product, but neither has explained the whole system. To investigate, ask which decision you are talking about and what evidence reveals how that decision was made.

Look before you explain

An output is what a system produces. A mechanism is how it produces that output. Seeing a red number establishes its colour; it does not reveal where the number came from.

Similar outputs can come from different mechanisms, and one product can contain several mechanisms.

One product, three different decisions

Follow the number, the colour and the driver’s message separately.

Read the whole system

Explain each decision

1. Predict a number

Earlier journeys were used to fit the delay model. It uses today’s route and traffic information to estimate a 12-minute delay. A prediction can be wrong.

2. Apply a written rule

A person wrote this instruction: turn the display red when the estimated delay is greater than 10 minutes. Twelve is greater than ten, so the display turns red.

3. Make a human choice

The driver can choose a replacement message, such as ROUTE CLOSED. That replacement is a human decision.

Trace an information path

  1. INPUT a command, signal, photo, sound, or number

  2. PROCESS a human choice, fixed instruction, or learned pattern

  3. OUTPUT movement, calculation, action, or prediction

Keep this important distinction

AI can also reason using knowledge and rules. So “written rule” does not always mean “outside AI.” In this bus case, changing the colour is a simple automatic step. Classify each decision separately.

CORE IDEA Look for evidence of how the system reasons or learns, not just its label or screen.

UNDER THE HOOD A system should be classified by its decision mechanism. Human control, fixed automation, and model-based prediction can produce similar visible behavior, so appearance and marketing words are weak evidence.

A closer explanation

In the first row, fitting means adjusting a model using examples. Applying the fitted model to a new journey is a different step. The model can produce an incorrect estimate even if it was trained carefully.

In the second row, a written threshold compares the estimate with 10. It can follow its instructions perfectly while using a bad estimate. In the third row, a person makes the message decision.

Connect the picture and the explanation

AI is a broader field than learning from examples. It also includes approaches that represent knowledge and reason with rules. Therefore, do not use the shortcut that every rule is non-AI.

In this case, a simple colour switch is ordinary automation; identifying that switch does not classify every other part of the bus system.

Watch the Reasoning, Not Just the Answer

Every step connects a claim to observable evidence.

CASE A remote-control car, calculator, motion light, smart speaker, photo sorter, and robot vacuum may all look clever, but they do not make decisions in the same way.

STEP 1 — OBSERVE a command, signal, photo, sound, or number

STEP 2 — TRACE a human choice, fixed instruction, or learned pattern

STEP 3 — CONCLUDE movement, calculation, action, or prediction

MODEL REASONING Follow the information and identify who or what makes the important choice. A robot body, screen, voice, or the word smart cannot prove AI.

If two mechanisms fit the evidence, say that more information is needed.

DO NOT OVERCLAIM Do not call a system AI because it looks, moves, or talks like a person.

What new evidence could change this answer?

A closer explanation

Question: Does the red display prove that a learned model was used? First separate the claim from the observation. The observation is that the displayed number is red. The claim concerns the process that produced the number.

Next find relevant evidence. The engineering note says earlier journeys were used to fit the delay estimator. That note supports a learned-model classification for the estimator. The red colour alone does not.

ClaimRelevant evidenceConclusion
The estimator uses a learned modelThe note describes fitting on past journeysSupported for the estimator
The red colour proves learningOnly the displayed colour is observedNot supported by colour alone
The driver override is a model predictionA driver selects the replacement messageContradicted in this case

A strong answer states its limit: I can identify these three mechanisms from the supplied note, but I cannot infer the model's accuracy from one estimate.

Even if the bus arrives exactly 12 minutes late, one success does not establish that the estimator will always work.

Learn from the worked case

Cover the result before checking it. Say what is given, choose the procedure, and follow one step at a time. Then uncover the result and explain your first mismatch. Ask why a step is allowed, not only what number it produces.

Repair a tempting explanation

A robot body, a screen or a correct answer does not reveal the decision mechanism.

Try two repairs

  • CLAIM A Every robot uses AI.

  • CLAIM B Anything with a screen is AI.

STRONG ANSWER FRAME I claim ______ because the evidence shows ______. The process is ______. One limit is ______.

What if the mechanism is unknown?

A robot vacuum is advertised as smart. The advertisement tells you almost nothing about its decisions. A useful question is not Does it look intelligent?

Ask instead how it selects a route, whether adjustable values were fitted using examples, or when a person supplies a command. It may combine several answers.

Sometimes the honest conclusion is that the mechanism is unknown. That is a precise answer when you identify the missing evidence. It is weaker to guess a mechanism and then treat your guess as a fact.

Keep observations, supplied evidence and assumptions separate in your investigation.
