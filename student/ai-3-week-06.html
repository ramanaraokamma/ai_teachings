The three roles

An example demonstrates behavior such as an answer’s structure. Approved evidence supplies the facts for the current task. A rubric lists observable conditions for judging the result. These roles are different: copying a neat two-field example does not establish a new answer’s truth. Decide the criteria and mandatory conditions before seeing candidate answers.

The complete frog-card contract

Use approved note N: a frog is an amphibian. Create a card about a frog with exactly Question and Answer fields. It must contain one question about the frog and a source-supported answer, with at most20 whitespace-separated words across the field contents, excluding the field labels. Every one of these four criteria is mandatory for a full pass. Format-only example: Question: What is a fox? Answer: A mammal.

It demonstrates the two-field layout; its fox facts are not approved evidence for the frog task.

A next-token example has a prefix and an observed next token. Suppose two held-out examples have observed target ready. Model A assigns target probabilities 0.8 and 0.5. Model B assigns 0.6 and 0.6.

A token-level objective rewards probability assigned to the observed targets. It does not directly inspect whether a completed answer follows a permission boundary or correctly cites a source.

Observed text may contain mistakes, omissions or unsuitable content. A model fitting it can learn those patterns. Provenance and permitted use matter alongside optimisation.

Training extension · Loss definition and complete two-example table

For target probability p>0, loss is -ln(p), using the natural logarithm. Since ln(1)=0, assigning probability 1 gives zero loss. Smaller positive probabilities give larger loss.

Probability zero for the observed target gives unbounded loss in the mathematical definition; do not silently substitute a small value without documenting a separate numerical policy.

ModelTarget probabilitiesMean target loss
A0.8, 0.5(-ln(.8)-ln(.5))/2 ≈ 0.458145
B0.6, 0.6-ln(.6) ≈ 0.510826

Model A has lower mean loss on these two examples. Multiplying probabilities gives 0.4 for A and 0.36 for B; taking the negative log of that product and dividing by two gives the same average.

These examples are separate target predictions, not automatically one continuous generated sequence.

Training extension · Averaging units and split contamination

If one sequence has three target tokens and another one, averaging four token losses weights the long sequence more. Averaging the two sequence means weights the sequences equally.

Both calculations can be defined, but they are different metrics. State the denominator before comparing. Here the mean is over two target examples.

Duplicate or related text can leak between splits. A copied paragraph in training and evaluation weakens an independence claim even if file names differ. Record grouping, deduplication and selection choices.

After tuning a model on evaluation scores, use a fresh reserved final evaluation for the selected procedure.
