Evidence limitAgreement in a small group is an observation, not a guarantee for an individual, another score bin or a new population.

Read the group interpretation and fictional scope

If many comparable cases receive probability .8, a calibrated model should produce positives in roughly 80 percent of those cases over adequate data.

It does not mean every case is 80 percent correct or that exactly eight of each next ten must be positive. Calibration is assessed across groups.

Compare average probability with observed positive frequency

Keep score, outcome count and denominator visible.

Complete two-group supplied example

In a fictional held-out check, ten cases all have predicted probability .2 and two are positive. Another ten all have probability .8 and six are positive.

Average predictions are .2 and .8; observed frequencies are 2/10=.2 and 6/10=.6. The second group is overconfident for positives in this sample.

Optional complete binning program

# Fictional score p(positive), observed binary outcome. Bins declared in advance.
rows = [(f'L{i:02}', 0.2, int(i <= 2)) for i in range(1, 11)]
rows += [(f'M{i:02}', 0.5, int(i <= 7)) for i in range(1, 11)]
rows += [(f'H{i:02}', 0.8, int(i <= 6)) for i in range(1, 11)]
bins = [('low', 0.0, 0.35), ('middle', 0.35, 0.65), ('high', 0.65, 1.0)]
for name, left, right in bins:
    selected = [(p, y) for item_id, p, y in rows
                if left <= p and (p < right or right == 1.0 and p <= right)]
    if not selected:
        print(name, 'no observations')
        continue
    mean_score = sum(p for p, y in selected) / len(selected)
    positives = sum(y for p, y in selected)
    frequency = positives / len(selected)
    print(name, len(selected), round(mean_score, 3), positives, round(frequency, 3))
# These groups describe p(positive), not the correctness rate of chosen labels.
# No calibrator is fitted, and no population guarantee follows from ten cases/bin.
