Understand RAG and grounding

Retrieval augmented generation, or RAG, first retrieves relevant records and then uses them as context for a generated answer. Grounding means connecting each factual claim to evidence that actually supports it. A retrieved passage can be irrelevant, stale, incomplete or misused. Citations make checking possible; they do not perform the check for us. This lesson traces supplied paper records, not a live model.

A grounded answer connects each factual claim to evidence actually retrieved for the task. Retrieval success does not automatically make generation faithful.

Extension · Chunking and ranking preview

Chunking divides documents into retrievable units. A useful chunk retains enough structure to interpret a passage, including headers and exceptions.

Lexical retrieval matches terms; vector retrieval compares numerical representations. Each can fail when the representation misses the relevant distinction.
