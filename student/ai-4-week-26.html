A team declares seeds 11, 22, 33 before running. Validation accuracies are 14/20, 16/20, 12/20:70, 80, 60 percent. Mean accuracy is 70 percent, with a 20-percentage-point range.

Reporting only seed 22 as the model result hides the declared experiment.

These three classifier results are supplied fictional records. A seed alone cannot recreate the unknown classifier, data or environment; the complete model-training procedure is not supplied. Replaying saved-output arithmetic differs from reproducing model training.

The separate complete A/Bweighted-score program below can actually be rerun with its fixed inputs and two weight versions; it has no random step, so its seed field is not applicable. A changed preference score is not an accuracy improvement.

Complete rerunnable versioned scoring program

items = [{"name": "A", "fun": 4, "learning": 1},
         {"name": "B", "fun": 2, "learning": 4}]
for version, fun_weight, learning_weight in [("v1", 2, 1), ("v2", 1, 2)]:
    scores = []
    for item in items:
        scores.append(fun_weight * item["fun"]
                      + learning_weight * item["learning"])
    print(version, scores)

Trace v1/v2scores and changed policy

The ranking changes because the preference weights changed. That is a policy comparison, not proof that v2 is more accurate.

Keep the same data and constraints when comparing weights, and record why a policy better fits the stated goal.

For the worked records, v1 gives A a score of 2 times 4 plus 1, or 9, and B a score of 2 times 2 plus 4, or 8. v2 gives A 4 plus 2 times 1, or 6, and B 2 plus 2 times 4, or 10. The winner changes because the weights changed.

Keep the records fixed so this explanation can be checked. A different preference policy is not automatically a more accurate model.

Optional complete saved-output summary program

# Complete synthetic saved-output record for arithmetic replay, not model training.
references = ['clear' if i % 2 else 'blocked' for i in range(1, 21)]
predictions = {}
for seed, mistakes in [(11, 6), (22, 4), (33, 8)]:
    predictions[seed] = [('blocked' if y == 'clear' else 'clear') if i < mistakes else y
                         for i, y in enumerate(references)]
accuracies = []
for seed in [11, 22, 33]:
    correct = sum(y == p for y, p in zip(references, predictions[seed]))
    accuracies.append(correct / len(references))
    print(seed, correct, len(references))
print('mean', round(sum(accuracies) / len(accuracies), 6))
print('range percentage points', round(100 * (max(accuracies) - min(accuracies)), 6))
# Seeds label supplied run records here; no classifier or random process is rerun.
