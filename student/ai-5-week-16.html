Notice the evidence

Investigate the supplied Regularisation and early stopping case. Separate observations, given facts and assumptions before choosing an explanation.

Keep h = ReLU(x), y = vh, train only v on (1, 2) and (2, 4), and freeze all hidden parameters and the zero output bias. Training MSE is L(v) = 2.5(v - 2) squared.

Define J(v) = L(v) + lambda times v squared, with lambda nonnegative. Only this one output weight is penalised here.

For lambda = 0.5, the penalty at v = 1.5 is 0.5 times 2.25 = 1.125. Prediction MSE is 0.625, so total objective J is 1.75. Keep these quantities separate in logs.

A smaller total objective does not automatically mean a smaller prediction error.

Student lesson · 3 of 18

See how it works

Follow the mechanism for Regularisation and early stopping. Connect each labelled input, deciding step and result.

The data gradient is 5(v - 2). The penalty gradient is 2 times lambda times v, giving total gradient 5(v - 2) + 2 lambda v. At v = 1.5 and lambda = 0.5, these contributions are -2.5 and 1.5, so total gradient is -1.

A rate 0.1 step gives v = 1.6.

def terms(v, penalty):
    data_loss = 2.5 * (v - 2) ** 2
    weight_cost = penalty * v * v
    gradient = 5 * (v - 2) + 2 * penalty * v
    return data_loss, weight_cost, data_loss + weight_cost, gradient

v = 1.5
penalty = 0.5
old = terms(v, penalty)
v = v - 0.1 * old[3]
print(round(v, 6))  # 1.6
print([round(x, 6) for x in terms(v, penalty)])
# [0.4, 1.28, 1.68, -0.4]

Assume finite numeric values and nonnegative penalty. At the new v, data loss is 0.4, penalty 1.28 and total 1.68.

Choose penalty strength using a declared validation prediction metric, not by comparing total objectives with different lambdas as if they were the same measure.

Why this result follows

Why the result follows

Regularisation adds a parameter penalty to the training objective. Here it discourages large a, so the objective balances prediction error against that penalty.

Early stopping is a different decision based on the supplied validation sequence. A tied validation loss is not improvement under this policy; stop when the patience allowance is used and restore the best checkpoint.

What this example does not establish

Regularisation and early stopping are different tools; neither guarantees better performance in every dataset.

Find and repair a mistake

Find the unsupported step in the Regularisation and early stopping claim and explain a repair using the supplied evidence.

Check the claim against the facts

Early stopping limits the training duration using development evidence; it does not add a weight term to the objective. For this policy, initialise the best checkpoint to epoch 0 and the miss count to zero.

After each later evaluation, save and reset only on a strict improvement; otherwise increment the count. Stop as soon as the count reaches patience, then reload the best checkpoint.

Check the claim against the facts

Suppose a pre-recorded demonstration also lists epoch 7 loss 1.3. A live run following the rule has already stopped at 6 and does not observe 7. Do not use future values to rewrite the stopping decision.

The policy can miss a later improvement; patience balances waiting against a training budget and must be specified in advance.

Check the claim against the facts

Save actual model parameters with the checkpoint, not just its epoch number or loss. With a fixed maximum epoch budget, stop when either limit is reached and restore the best observed state.

An exact tie keeps the earlier checkpoint under our rule. Different minimum-improvement thresholds are possible, but would be different declared policies.
