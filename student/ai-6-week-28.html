Main lesson · Section 2

Connect the field, error and recovery action

A status mark is useful only when its meaning and next step are available.

Begin with an unexplained red border

Imagine a room form draws a red border around a box and says nothing else. A learner may see that something changed yet still not know the problem or how to fix it. Give the field a visible label, describe the error in words, connect that description to the field and provide a usable next action.

Check the whole task with a keyboard and relevant assistive technology rather than assuming that a colourful redesign solved it.

Define accessible feedback

Accessibility makes information and controls usable across stated needs. Feedback should describe the problem and recovery action without exposing sensitive details.

    The picture is a separate answer-feedback example. The original red circle gains the text “More explanation needed”, a labelled focusable next-action link and programmatic status information. These additions address different barriers: words communicate meaning, a usable link offers an action and programmatic information supports assistive-technology access.

    Audit the complete task rather than one visual property

    Record conditions and unresolved checks honestly.

    Core accessibility qualification and failure analysis

    Accessibility requirements need real interaction checks and appropriate user feedback; a checklist alone is incomplete.

    A useful check asks what would make the reasoning fail. Distinguish a failure to execute the declared procedure from a weakness in the procedure itself.

    The first may require a correction; the second requires an explicitly revised method and new evidence. Preserve unsuccessful cases so an improved result cannot hide the original problem.
