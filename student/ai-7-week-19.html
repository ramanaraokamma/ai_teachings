Student lesson · 2 of 16

Notice the evidence

Research ethics considers purpose, burden, voluntary participation and consequences. Approval to teach does not automatically authorise a research data collection.

Begin by writing the question the procedure is intended to answer. Identify the information already supplied, the information it will produce and the information still missing. These are constructed classroom examples; they do not require live accounts, private records or an external service. Do not treat a plausible explanation as a measured outcome.

See how it works

Use fictional records for this course. For the supplied classroom policy, a real study needs responsible review, plain-language purpose, opt-in consent and withdrawal handling before collection. Do not implement real recruitment in this exercise.

Before applying the procedure, write its units, denominator or decision boundary where relevant. A partner should be able to repeat each step using the same inputs. If a required input is absent or violates the stated contract, record that condition explicitly instead of quietly replacing it with a convenient value.

A pictorial worked case: Research ethics and consent

Consent is specific, not universal. A fictional study asks adults to submit anonymous drawings for one pattern-recognition investigation.

The facts we will use

Consent covers this study only. It excludes public posting of originals and unrelated face-recognition research.

Why this result follows

Research ethics includes what people agree to, how they can decline and how risks are addressed. A contribution does not grant unlimited reuse.

What this example does not establish

This fictional adult scenario is not approval for research with children; real oversight and applicable rules are separate requirements.

Read the labelled picture: Identify which uses the drawing consent permits and excludes. Can the unrelated project reuse the drawings under this agreement, and what new authorisation would be needed?

Find and repair a mistake

This classroom policy exercise does not determine real legal or institutional requirements.

A useful check asks what would make the reasoning fail. Distinguish a failure to execute the declared procedure from a weakness in the procedure itself. The first may require a correction; the second requires an explicitly revised method and new evidence. Preserve unsuccessful cases so an improved result cannot hide the original problem.
