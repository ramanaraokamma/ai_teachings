The same numbers can express different situations when grouped differently.

Read the physical counting contract

Three packs each contain eight counters, with two more loose counters. First count the packed counters, then add the loose ones. A program needs that structure, not just the right numbers.

    Separate practice · Same-precedence operations
    print(20 - 6 - 2)
print(20 - (6 - 2))
print(12 / 3 * 2)

    The first line groups subtraction from left to right: (20 - 6) - 2 gives 12. The second gives 16. Multiplication and division share a precedence level, so 12 / 3 * 2 groups as (12 / 3) * 2 and displays 8.0, not 2.0.

    Ordinary / division produces a float, even when the result is mathematically whole.

    Main lesson · Section 7

    Choose division, complete groups or a remainder

    The counting interpretation assumes nonnegative whole counters and a positive group size.

    Run the complete 17-in-groups-of-5 script

    counters = 17
group_size = 5
print(counters / group_size)
print(counters // group_size)
print(counters % group_size)

    Check 17=3*5+2 and the three output meanings

    The outputs are 3.4, 3 and 2. With nonnegative whole counters and a positive group size, // gives the number of complete groups and % gives the remainder: 17 = 3 * 5 + 2.

    Ordinary division answers a different question about a ratio; 3.4 complete five-counter groups cannot be built without splitting counters.

    State the zero/negative domain boundary

    For nonnegative whole inputs, the remainder is at least zero and smaller than the group size. A group size of zero is invalid and division by zero raises an error.

    These whole-group interpretations assume nonnegative counts and positive group sizes; do not generalise them to negative quantities without examining Python’s floor-division rule.
