Student lesson · 6 of 17

Follow a worked example

print(20 - 6 - 2)
print(20 - (6 - 2))
print(12 / 3 * 2)

The first line groups subtraction from left to right: (20 - 6) - 2 gives 12. The second gives 16. Multiplication and division share a precedence level, so 12 / 3 * 2 groups as (12 / 3) * 2 and displays 8.0, not 2.0. Ordinary / division produces a float, even when the result is mathematically whole.

Student lesson · 7 of 17

Find and repair a mistake

counters = 17
group_size = 5
print(counters / group_size)
print(counters // group_size)
print(counters % group_size)

The outputs are 3.4, 3 and 2. With nonnegative whole counters and a positive group size, // gives the number of complete groups and % gives the remainder: 17 = 3 * 5 + 2. Ordinary division answers a different question about a ratio; 3.4 complete five-counter groups cannot be built without splitting counters.

For nonnegative whole inputs, the remainder is at least zero and smaller than the group size. A group size of zero is invalid and division by zero raises an error. These whole-group interpretations assume nonnegative counts and positive group sizes; do not generalise them to negative quantities without examining Python’s floor-division rule.
