Main lesson · Section 2

Give calculation and source verification different jobs

The record supplies counts and a calendar fact, but no intended meeting date.

Understand numerator, denominator and validity

A precise-looking number can still use the wrong source, denominator or unit. A denominator is the total used in a fraction: 15 passes out of 20 trials means 15/20, not 15/15. Verify the source/run ID and counted population before calculating. A percentage is the fraction multiplied by 100. Calendar validity is a different check: an impossible date can be rejected without knowing what date its author intended.

Read the complete main record

Complete fictional main record: 15 of 20 model trials passed. The draft says 70% passed and announces a meeting on June 31. Calendar fact: June has 30 days. No authoritative meeting notice is supplied. Recompute the percentage from the record; identify the invalid date and request the intended meeting date. These invented counts do not establish that any real model was tested.

Main lesson · Section 7

Find the unsupported replacement

A detected error and a justified correction require different evidence.

Repair the calendar shortcut

Claim to repair: June 31 is impossible, so the meeting must be June 30. The calendar disproves June 31; it does not select June 30 rather than July 1 or another date. Ask the responsible organiser or obtain the approved notice. Similarly, correcting 15/20 to 75% does not prove that the source counts were measured reliably.
