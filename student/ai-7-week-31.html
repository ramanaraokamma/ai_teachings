Start with the research problem

A strong results paragraph first says what was measured, on which cases and with what counts. It then states the descriptive difference and the limits that matter for interpreting it. A support-score gain is not automatically a learning gain, and one extra pass is not proof of universal reliability.

Use limitations to identify the missing evidence precisely rather than adding a vague sentence that everything needs more research.

Original concept explanation

Results and limitations should state observed estimates before interpreting them. Separate measured comparisons from causal or deployment claims.

    Main lesson · Section 7

    Find the first unsupported inference and repair it

    Use the supplied conditions rather than inventing missing evidence.

    Original qualification and repair setup

    A limitation should directly bound the corresponding claim rather than contradict an oversized headline.

    A useful check asks what would make the reasoning fail. Distinguish a failure to execute the declared procedure from a weakness in the procedure itself.

    The first may require a correction; the second requires an explicitly revised method and new evidence. Preserve unsuccessful cases so an improved result cannot hide the original problem.
