Concept to explain

Week 19: Sampling and Representation

TermMeaning
sampleA selected group of observations, or an outcome drawn from a distribution; the context identifies which meaning applies.
populationThe full group of cases or people a study aims to describe.
representationA way to encode information so a person or system can work with it.
sampling biasA systematic mismatch between selected examples and the population the study intends to describe.

    W1 Define the population

    The question is whether all Grade 7 students prefer a longer lunch. Name the population and one unsuitable sample.

    Population: all Grade 7 students. Only lunch-club members or one friend group is an unsuitable basis for a whole-grade claim.

    W2 Read the sample result

    Seven of 12 fictional respondents prefer longer lunch. State the result without generalizing.

    7/12 of these respondents prefer longer lunch, about 58.3 percent.

    The numerator is seven respondents choosing longer lunch and the denominator is all twelve observed respondents. The fraction is 7/12, approximately 58.3 percent.

    Say “of these respondents”; the calculation alone supplies no evidence about the preferences of students who were not represented.

    W3 Repair a claim

    A report says all Grade 7 prefers coding after surveying only the coding club. Rewrite it.

    Among surveyed coding-club respondents, the observed choices favored coding; the result does not establish the whole grade preference.

    W5 Separate invitation and response

    Twenty students are selected fairly but only 8 answer. Does fair selection remove the need to report nonresponse?

    No. The 12 nonrespondents may differ. Report 8 of 20 and the limitation.

    Extension questions and keys

    Graphs and honest scales — W1 Read the scale

    A bar is 6 squares long with 2 votes per square. How many votes is that?

    12 votes.

    One square represents two votes. Six equal squares therefore represent 6 times 2 = 12 votes. Ask the learner to name both the visible length and the unit scale before giving the count.

    Graphs and honest scales — W2 Compare fairly

    Counts are 12 and 15. State the difference and ratio using the true values.

    Difference 3; ratio 15/12 = 1.25.

    The count difference is 15 - 12 = 3 votes. Taking the larger count relative to the smaller gives 15/12 = 1.25, so 15 is 1.25 times 12, or 25 percent larger. Label which count is the reference for the ratio.

    Graphs and honest scales — W3 Repair a misleading graph

    Bars for 12 and 15 start at 10. Explain the misleading lengths and repair.

    Visible lengths 2 and 5 suggest a 2.5 ratio. Start at zero, use a common scale and label counts 12 and 15.
