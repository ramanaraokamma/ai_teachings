Explain and model the mechanism

A confounder changes alongside the intended factor and can offer another explanation. Controls aim to isolate the proposed mechanism.

Begin by writing the question the procedure is intended to answer. Identify the information already supplied, the information it will produce and the information still missing. These are constructed classroom examples; they do not require live accounts, private records or an external service. Do not treat a plausible explanation as a measured outcome.

For a toy comparison vary only retrieval method while holding corpus, queries, cutoff and relevance rubric fixed. Randomise or counterbalance order when order effects matter.

Before applying the procedure, write its units, denominator or decision boundary where relevant. A partner should be able to repeat each step using the same inputs. If a required input is absent or violates the stated contract, record that condition explicitly instead of quietly replacing it with a convenient value.

TermMeaning
ConfounderA differing condition offering another explanation
ControlA design constraint preserving comparison conditions
CounterbalancingVarying order to reduce systematic order differences

Guided: Corpus and case differences confound the method effect. Rerun matched inputs or report a whole-system comparison without isolation. Qualification: Controls address specified alternatives, not every possible unknown confounder. Exit: accept a concrete variation with its predicted outcome and a stated condition that would challenge the mechanism; reject a claim resting on unmeasured evidence.

A pictorial worked case: Confounders and controls

Separate background from class. Training data show every healthy leaf on white paper and every spotted leaf on blue paper.

The facts we will use

Goal is leaf condition, not paper colour. A proposed check uses both paper colours for each condition.

Why this result follows

A confounder can provide an alternative explanation for a result. Crossing conditions makes the intended feature and the nuisance feature less entangled.

What this example does not establish

A changed-background test can diagnose sensitivity; it does not prove the model’s complete internal reasoning.

Change one thing in the pictured case

Change: New cases still use only healthy-white and spotted-blue.

Prediction: The collection does not resolve the background confounding.

Reason: The same two variables remain coupled instead of being varied separately.

Ask while showing the picture

Cover the result. Ask learners to name the inputs and predict the next step. Uncover one step, connect the icon to the actual procedure, and explain its evidence. Then change the supplied condition and compare. The picture is a representation; it does not document execution of a real AI model.

Pictorial practice question

Draw the four condition/background combinations. Which variables were coupled before, and why would collecting only healthy-white and spotted-blue again leave the confounding unresolved?

Reasoned teacher answer

Condition and background change together in the original collection. Collect healthy-white, healthy-blue, spotted-white and spotted-blue cases. Compare predictions when the same leaf’s background changes under a controlled test. Changed case: The collection does not resolve the background confounding. Teacher guide · 4 of 6

Run the investigation and diagnose misconceptions

The worked result establishes every future case: Controls address specified alternatives, not every possible unknown confounder.

A missing input may be replaced without recording the change: A replacement changes the evidence or contract. Record the change and its justification; do not silently turn unknown into a measured value.

Repeating an assessed case is new independent evidence: A replay checks consistency. An example used for feedback or revision cannot later be described as untouched final evaluation.

Use Section 9 for the evidence ledger. Add a boundary or missing-input case and record the expected response before checking. More successful repeats of one case do not establish wider coverage.

Feedback that the learner uses

Name the earliest unsupported step and give one action to repair it. Have the learner make that repair now and explain why. Then assess the relevant skill on a fresh, fully specified case.
