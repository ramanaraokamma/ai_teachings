Main lesson · Section 2

Teach claim-level checks, units and source origins

Keep the two ribbon datasets distinct and reserve the mass case for independent work.

Read card R and split its draft into claims

An answer can contain a mixture of correct, incorrect and unverified statements. Checking one sentence does not validate every other sentence. First separate the answer into individual claims.

Then ask what evidence would support each one: a record, a measurement, a calculation or some combination.

Our fictional ribbon activity uses current materials card R. R states: four rolls of ribbon, each exactly 2 metres long; twelve pieces will each use 50 centimetres; no cutting loss is included in this paper model.

One metre equals 100 centimetres. An old card Q describes a different activity with three rolls. These are provided classroom records, not instructions to cut real materials.

A draft says “R gives four rolls. The starting length is 8 metres. The pieces use 600 metres. Two metres remain. Every participant will enjoy the activity.” Do not approve or reject the whole paragraph at once.

Inspect the roll count, starting length, used length, remaining length and enjoyment claim separately.

Inspect citation support and relevance

A citation is useful only if you can locate the source and confirm that it supports the specific claim. The title “Materials card R” beside a paragraph does not make the enjoyment claim part of R.

If a cited card Z is not supplied or cannot be found, that citation cannot currently verify its claim. Record the gap rather than inventing Z’s contents.

Check whether the source covers the right activity, version, population or time. Q’s three rolls may be accurate for its own activity but irrelevant to the current four-roll task.

In real work, also inspect who created the record, how they obtained the information and whether there is a correction or better source.

Model two routes with consistent units

QuantityCalculation in centimetresEquivalent in metres
Starting length4 × 200 cm = 800 cm8 m
Length used12 × 50 cm = 600 cm6 m
Length remaining800 cm − 600 cm = 200 cm2 m

Convert to one length unit before subtracting. Two metres is 200 centimetres, so four rolls give 800 centimetres. The twelve pieces use 600 centimetres, leaving 200 centimetres, or 2 metres.

Subtracting the bare numbers 8 − 600 would mix metres with centimetres and would not describe the remaining length correctly.

Use a second route as a check: 50 centimetres is half a metre, so twelve pieces use six metres. Eight minus six leaves two metres.

A reasonableness check also asks whether the used length is at most the starting length and whether starting length equals used plus remaining.

These checks can reveal mistakes; they do not replace checking the original inputs and assumptions.

Introduce the separate bookmark facts

A fictional craft sheet calculates ribbon left after making bookmarks.

Four rolls each contain exactly 150 cm. Eight bookmarks use exactly 50 cm each.

No other ribbon loss is assumed; no competition result is recorded.

Inspect the labelled quantity comparison

Trace the bookmark audit

All arithmetic first uses centimetres. Converting only after subtraction avoids mixing units. The record supports available, used and remaining lengths under its no-loss assumption. It does not support a promise about winning.

A numerically correct total elsewhere in a draft cannot rescue a wrong unit or an invented claim.

Declare the model’s limits

Real cutting losses or inaccurate roll lengths would change this toy calculation; the assumption must be declared.

Change the bookmark count

Change: Make ten bookmarks instead of eight.

Prediction: 100 cm, or 1 m, remains.

Reason: 600−10×50=100 cm.

CORE IDEA Fluent AI output is not evidence; important claims need independent support.

UNDER THE HOOD Verification is claim-level evidence work. Source independence, authority, direct support, recency, and agreement matter. Multiple pages repeating the same upstream error are not independent confirmation.

Draw a fresh example with all three stages:

Every step connects a claim to observable evidence.

STEP 1 — OBSERVE specific claim extracted from an output

STEP 2 — TRACE check independent suitable sources and reconcile differences

STEP 3 — CONCLUDE supported, corrected, uncertain, or removed claim

MODEL REASONING Check the exact claim, not the whole paragraph. Use sources with direct relevant evidence, note publication context, and explain whether they independently agree.

If evidence is weak or conflicting, revise or remove the claim.

DO NOT OVERCLAIM Two search results are not two independent sources when both copy the same page.

Assess the selected new case

Keep this key until after the first attempt; accept valid alternatives with defensible reasoning.

Selected independent question and key

Selected case: Fictional craft card M states five sealed bags of modelling material at exactly 250 g per bag. Three models each use 300 g, with no other loss.

A draft says “M supplies 1250 g; the models use 900 g; 350 kg remains; every model will win a prize.” Check all four claims, show the remaining mass in grams, and explain the status of the prize claim. Use 1 kg = 1000 g.

1250 g is supported by 5 × 250 g; 900 g is supported by 3 × 300 g. Remaining mass is 1250 − 900 = 350 g, so 350 kg is contradicted by the inputs and unit conversion: 350 kg would be 350000 g, greater than the starting mass.

The prize claim is unresolved because M gives no prize evidence; it is not established true or false by the material counts.

W1 Separate source facts from derived quantities

Using R, identify a directly stated fact and a quantity that must be calculated. Give the source or expression for each. Explain why a citation to R does not verify the claim that every participant will enjoy the activity.

A directly stated fact is four rolls, 2 m per roll or twelve 50 cm pieces. A derived quantity is 8 m starting length, 6 m used or 2 m remaining, supported by the relevant calculation from R.

R contains no enjoyment evidence, so citing it does not support everyone enjoying the activity.

W2 Repair a unit error

A report calculates 12 × 50 = 600 and labels the result metres. Use R to correct the unit, convert the result to metres and calculate the remaining length.

Give a second arithmetic route or a check using starting, used and remaining lengths.

Each piece is 50 cm, so 12 × 50 cm = 600 cm = 6 m. Starting length is 4 × 2 m = 8 m; remaining is 8 − 6 = 2 m. A check is 6 m used + 2 m remaining = 8 m starting; alternatively twelve half-metre pieces use 6 m.

W3 Do not count copied claims as separate evidence

Three posters say 600 metres were used, but all copied one draft. A fourth poster cites card Z, which is unavailable. Explain why these posters do not settle the quantity.

Name the available evidence and check you would use instead.

The first three are copies of one claim, not independent evidence. Z cannot be inspected, so the fourth citation cannot currently verify the result.

Inspect supplied R and recompute twelve 50 cm pieces as 600 cm or 6 m; verify source, units and the no-loss assumption.

W5 State what a missing source prevents you from knowing

Another draft claims that the amount left in W4 accounts for all real-world waste, citing a missing instruction card. Explain which result can still be checked from M and which claim remains unresolved.

What information would be needed before revising the no-loss model?

The no-other-loss model in M can be checked: 350 g remains under its stated assumptions. The assertion that this includes all real-world waste is unresolved without relevant waste information.

Obtain the actual instruction or measurement record specifying additional loss, its units and what stage it covers; do not invent a waste amount or subtract it twice.

05. Connect the explanation to its limit

All arithmetic first uses centimetres. Converting only after subtraction avoids mixing units. The record supports available, used and remaining lengths under its no-loss assumption. It does not support a promise about winning.

A numerically correct total elsewhere in a draft cannot rescue a wrong unit or an invented claim. Limit: Real cutting losses or inaccurate roll lengths would change this toy calculation; the assumption must be declared.
