Concept to explain

A controlled improvement changes one design choice and reruns the same tests.

TermMeaning
controlled improvementA revision whose effect is checked under comparable conditions rather than assumed from a changed result.
before/afterResults recorded prior to and following a change; other changes can affect the comparison.
tradeoffA situation where improving one desired property can make another harder to achieve.
regressionIn testing, a new failure introduced by a change; in modelling, prediction of a numeric target. Use the meaning indicated by the lesson.

Explanations for the additional workbook examples

W1 Locate the cause

Why does minutes < limit reject a valid exactly-at-limit activity?

Strict less-than excludes equality; at most requires <=.

If minutes equals limit, minutes < limit evaluates to False because the comparison is strict. The phrase at most includes equality, so the validated-duration predicate must be minutes <= limit. Test below, at and above the limit to reveal this difference.

W2 Test neighbors

For limit 15, give expected eligibility at 14, 15 and 16.

True, True, False.

With limit 15, compare each value using <=: 14 <= 15 is True, 15 <= 15 is True, and 16 <= 15 is False. Keep equality visible as the boundary case rather than testing only clearly short and clearly long activities.

W3 Reject a bad fix

A proposed fix accepts minutes <= limit + 1. Give a counterexample at limit 15.

It accepts 16, which should be excluded.

Set minutes to 16 and limit to 15. The proposed condition checks 16 <= 16 and accepts it, but the requirement checks 16 <= 15 and rejects it. This single counterexample demonstrates that adding one to the limit changed the policy rather than repairing it.

Use minutes <= limit after validation. Expectations True, True, False, True. Include a deterministic tie case and a schema-invalid row that never reaches selection; state expected outcomes.
