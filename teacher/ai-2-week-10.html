Explain the added worked example

Your task is to build a paper classifier that another learner can operate without guessing your instructions. It will predict short or tall for imaginary towers. Use the threshold of 6 centimetres selected after adding the height 5 short example in Week 13. Name this selected model Tower1.

Write an input contract: one height recorded as a whole number of centimetres from 1 through 10, including both endpoints. Require the unit cm to be shown. This is a classroom design choice about what the procedure accepts. It is not a claim that every accepted height appeared in fitting or that towers outside this range cannot exist.

A complete classifier needs instructions for checking the input, applying Tower1 when the input is accepted, and recording the outcome. It must also say what happens when the input fails a check. The threshold alone does not provide these surrounding instructions.

For every record, first check that a height is present and its stated unit is cm. Then check that it is a whole number from 1 through 10. If any check fails, record no prediction and the reason, then stop processing that record. Do not continue into the threshold comparison.

If all checks pass, compare the height with 6. At least 6 gives the prediction tall; otherwise give short. Record the original input, model version Tower1 and the prediction. For a rejected record, record the original input, Tower1, no prediction and a reason. Tower1 identifies the intended model even when its threshold was not used.

A valid 9 cm input receives a tall prediction, but the input check does not establish that the prediction matches its comparison label. Validation checks the contract. Evaluation checks predictions against appropriate evidence. Keep these questions separate.

Sixty millimetres equals six centimetres, but this version accepts cm records only. It does not contain a conversion step. Diagnose and reteach

For guessing, return to given facts and request a trace. For vocabulary without application, use one example and one non-example. For arithmetic errors, check the setup before the calculation. Finish with a different uncoached case before recording mastery.

Address these additional misconceptions

Any numeric input must receive a category: Check the stated unit, whole-number condition and supported range before comparing the threshold.

An accepted input proves a correct prediction: Ask separately whether the input satisfies the contract and whether the prediction agrees with evaluation evidence.

No prediction means a third category of tower: It is a system outcome indicating that no short or tall category was assigned.

Writing a rejection is enough even if processing continues: Follow the failed path to an explicit stop so the later rule cannot overwrite the outcome.

Explanations for the additional workbook examples

W1 Write an input contract

Write the complete Tower1 input contract. Give one accepted example and three different rejected examples, each with its reason. Explain why an accepted input does not have to be one of the examples used to fit the threshold.

Require a present height, unit cm, a whole number and range 1 through 10 inclusive. For example, 7 cm is accepted; blank is missing, 60 mm has the wrong unit and 11 cm is outside the range. Fractional values such as 4.5 cm also fail. The contract defines accepted inputs; it does not list only the fitting records or establish prediction correctness.

W2 Construct both outcome paths

Write numbered instructions for Tower1, including input checks, the stopping path after a failed check, the threshold rule and the record to keep. Your partner must be able to use them without supplying an unstated rule.

Check present height and cm unit, then whole number in 1 through 10. On failure, record original input, Tower1, no prediction and the reason; stop that record. Otherwise predict tall if height is at least 6, short otherwise. Record original input, Tower1 and prediction. Equivalent clear branches are acceptable.

W3 Trace boundaries and a broken path

Trace 1 cm, 5 cm, 6 cm, 10 cm and 11 cm using Tower1. A faulty procedure records no prediction for a blank, then continues and treats the blank as zero to report short. Identify the missing instruction and the proper final outcome.

The first four are accepted and predict short, short, tall and tall. At 11 cm reject for being outside the range. The faulty path lacks stopping after failure; a blank must end as no prediction with reason missing height. It must not be converted to zero or reach the threshold rule.

W4 Build an independent ribbon classifier

Design Ribbon1 for ribbon lengths recorded as whole centimetres from 1 through 12 inclusive, with unit cm required. Predict long at 8 cm or more and short otherwise. Write complete instructions and trace 7 cm, 8 cm, a blank length and 13 cm. Include reasons for no prediction and the model version in each record.

Check present length, cm unit, whole number and range 1 through 12; otherwise record no prediction with reason and stop. For accepted inputs, use at least 8 for long, otherwise short. Ribbon1 at 7 cm predicts short; at 8 cm predicts long; blank gives no prediction because length is missing; 13 cm gives no prediction because outside range. Each record includes original input, Ribbon1 and outcome, plus reason when rejected.

W5 Explain what your procedure establishes

Ribbon1 accepts 12 cm and predicts long. No comparison label has been supplied for that record. State what has been checked and what remains unknown. Explain why changing a blank to 1 cm just to produce a category would be a mistake.

The value satisfies the input contract and the stated rule predicts long. Agreement with an appropriate comparison label has not been checked. Replacing a blank with 1 invents a measurement, conceals missing information and violates the stated rejection path. Ask for the measurement instead.

Selected case: A written rule selects science books with fewer than 140 pages. Trace science/139, science/140, history/90 and missing-genre/90. Policy: missing required information means request it.

Selected case answer

Select science/139. Reject science/140 at the strict boundary and history/90 on genre. Request the missing genre rather than inventing it. Both conditions are required.

Feedback that the learner uses

Name the earliest unsupported step and give one action to repair it. Have the learner make that repair now and explain why. Then assess the relevant skill on a fresh, fully specified case. Confirm it was not previously practised with feedback; solve and check the new case before assigning it. A follow-up design prompt is a starting brief, not automatically an equivalent test.

Use feedback, labs and repair paths

Use the two formative questions as supported practice, not as independent mastery scores. Discuss the deciding evidence rather than accepting a guessed selection.

The feedback comes from the reviewed public worked case. Do not reveal the separately reserved independent teacher answer until the learner has attempted that case.

Ask students to predict before running code. Preserve errors and actual outputs; collect a changed test and an explanation. The branch-boundary and indexing lessons deliberately include failure cases.

Use the earlier worked-explanation links to address a specific missing building block, then return to the current question. Adapt this suggestion after observing the learner; it is not an automatic diagnosis.
