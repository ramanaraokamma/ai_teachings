Retrieval answer guidance

Fair for Whom?

Without opening the old lesson, explain one key idea from Fair for Whom?. Give an example and a mistake that the idea helps you avoid.

Look for: Compute a correct-out-of-assessed fraction and explain its denominator

Reference example, not the only acceptable wording: A has 9 correct out of 12 assessed: 9/12 = 3/4 = 75%. The denominator includes all 12 judged outcomes, including 3 incorrect ones. Using 9/9 would exclude the errors and falsely report perfect assessed accuracy.

Good Data, Bad Data

Without opening the old lesson, explain one key idea from Good Data, Bad Data. Give an example and a mistake that the idea helps you avoid.

Look for: Check records against a stated format, unit and meaning before using them

Reference example, not the only acceptable wording: Height 60 fails the whole-number range 1 through 10. Travel blank is missing; its value cannot be inferred. Travel 0 is valid in the whole-number range 0 through 10 and can represent observed no travel. Zero is a measurement, not a replacement for a blank.

Confidence Is Not Correctness

Without opening the old lesson, explain one key idea from Confidence Is Not Correctness. Give an example and a mistake that the idea helps you avoid.

Look for: Distinguish a confidence claim from evidence that a prediction was correct

Reference example, not the only acceptable wording: The learner replaced the documented score meaning with a probability interpretation. Report 80 of 100 feature-match points. To claim a chance of correctness, a defined probability method and relevant outcome evaluation would be needed.

Explain the mechanism

Explain the mechanism

Follow the information instead of imagining magic inside the machine.

INPUT candidate information fields

PROCESS keep only fields needed for the stated goal

OUTPUT useful result with reduced privacy risk

CORE IDEA A useful system should collect only information it truly needs.

UNDER THE HOOD Data minimization limits collection, access, retention, and reuse to what is necessary for a defined purpose. Less data reduces breach exposure and misuse, but essential high-stakes information still needs secure, consent-aware handling.

Draw a fresh example with all three stages:

Every step connects a claim to observable evidence.

CASE A lunch helper needs food preferences and allergy information for its task, not a face photo, home address, or family income.

STEP 1 — OBSERVE candidate information fields

STEP 2 — TRACE keep only fields needed for the stated goal

STEP 3 — CONCLUDE useful result with reduced privacy risk

MODEL REASONING Keep only information required for safety and stated preferences, allow a person to provide or update it, restrict access, and delete it when the purpose ends. Explain why each removed field adds risk without benefit.

DO NOT OVERCLAIM Useful does not mean permitted forever or for every new purpose.

Concrete teaching case

A genre-based book helper asks for genre, home address and a photograph.

Which fields are necessary?

Exact explanation to model

Genre is relevant; address and photo are unnecessary for the stated task. Collecting less reduces exposure.

Explain the added worked example

Privacy includes having appropriate boundaries around information about people. A useful first question is: what exact job are we trying to do? A second is: what is the least information that can do that job? Collecting extra details just because a form has room for them creates more information to protect.

Our fictional class wants to choose one of three workshop topics for Friday: drawing, music or coding. It needs a total for each topic, not a list of who chose what. Each fictional pupil gets one paper slip and marks one topic. No real class survey is needed for this lesson. The teacher counts the choices and uses the largest total; a tie goes to a class discussion.

A proposed form asks for name, full date of birth, home address, photograph and topic choice. For the stated counting task only the topic choice is needed. Remove the other fields before collection. Do not fill them in and promise to ignore them later. If the purpose changes to individual attendance, design and justify that separate task with the teacher rather than silently reusing the survey.

Suppose the fictional counts are drawing 8, music 7 and coding 5. These totals support the choice of drawing. There is no need to publish each slip or a photograph of a child holding it. The teacher checks the total of 20 against the number of slips and then follows the agreed school process for disposing of the slips after verification.

A retention plan needs a clear trigger and a responsible person: for example, the teacher disposes of the fictional vote slips after the totals are checked and the activity closes. Real classroom records must follow the school’s approved process. Deleting one visible copy does not prove that every backup, screenshot or previously shared copy is gone.

A code is not a promise of anonymity. If a teacher keeps a key connecting P07 to a pupil, the coded record can still be linked to that pupil. Even without a key, a combination such as an exact birthday, a team and a photograph may identify someone. Ask whether a reader could connect the information to a person, not just whether the name column is blank.

Totals also need context. A report saying that the only member of a tiny club chose music reveals that member’s choice. Our whole-class topic totals reveal less detail than individual rows, but we should not describe every possible total as automatically anonymous. Share only what the audience needs for the purpose.

A pictorial worked case: Privacy by Choosing Less

A fictional teacher wants to count broken craft tools by type, without contacting borrowers.

The facts we will use

Needed output: totals for scissors, ruler and hole-punch repairs.

Draft form requests pupil name, birthday, tool type and repair-needed Yes/No. No borrower follow-up is required.

Why this result follows

A field is justified by what the task needs to do, not by whether it might become useful someday. Names and birthdays do not help these repair totals. Replacing names with codes still leaves linked personal records if someone keeps a name-code key; collecting less can remove the need for that link entirely.

What this example does not establish

Removing these fields is not a universal guarantee of anonymity or permission to collect other identifying combinations.

Change one thing in the pictured case

Change: The teacher adds an authorised task to return one repaired tool to its borrower.

Prediction: A minimal contact mechanism may now be needed for that tool.

Reason: The purpose changed; reassess necessity rather than restoring every old field.

Ask while showing the picture

Cover the result. Ask learners to name the inputs and predict the next step. Uncover one step, connect the icon to the actual procedure, and explain its evidence. Then change the supplied condition and compare. The picture is a representation; it does not document execution of a real AI model.

Reduce help deliberately

Completion task: A teacher replaces names with codes and keeps a separate code-to-name key. A pupil calls the records anonymous. Explain the problem. Then explain why removing names while leaving photographs would not settle the question either.

Teacher check: The key can reconnect each code to a person, so the coded records are not anonymous just because names are absent. Restricting the key helps limit access but does not erase the link. Photographs can identify people even if the name field is removed.

Start by identifying the given inputs together. Leave the intermediate steps blank. If needed, model one step, then withdraw that help on the next step. Move to a full trace only when the partial trace is understood. For partners, alternate explainer and checker; collect a response from every learner before discussion.

Individual reasoning check

Use the worked case for Privacy and data minimisation. Proposed explanation: “More data always improves a task”. Decide whether this explanation is supported. Point to the step or supplied fact that decides; explain your repair if needed.

Expected correction: Unnecessary identity fields do not improve a category count and create more information to manage.

Collect explanations, not only yes/no votes. If a misunderstanding is common, re-model the relevant step for the class. If it affects a few learners, give a short targeted group explanation while others solve a more demanding variation.

Diagnose and reteach

CLAIM TO REPAIR Collecting extra personal details always improves a tool.

For guessing, return to given facts and request a trace. For vocabulary without application, use one example and one non-example. For arithmetic errors, check the setup before the calculation. Finish with a different uncoached case before recording mastery.

Address these additional misconceptions

More data always improves a task: Unnecessary identity fields do not improve a category count and create more information to manage.

Codes or missing names mean anonymous: A key or other identifying fields may reconnect records to a person.

Every total is safe to publish: A small or distinctive group may allow a reader to infer a person’s choice.

Deleting one copy proves all copies are gone: Other copies, screenshots or backups may remain; follow the adult-led school process.

Explanations for the additional workbook examples

W1 Trim a form to its purpose

The fictional workshop vote asks for name, full date of birth, address, photograph and topic choice. Its only purpose is to count votes for drawing, music and coding. State which field to keep, which to remove and why. Explain why collecting the extra fields first is different from never collecting them.

Keep topic choice; remove name, full date of birth, address and photograph. The totals do not require identity or contact details. Collecting extra fields creates copies and exposure to manage even if those fields are later ignored or deleted.

W2 Challenge a claim of anonymity

A teacher replaces names with codes and keeps a separate code-to-name key. A pupil calls the records anonymous. Explain the problem. Then explain why removing names while leaving photographs would not settle the question either.

The key can reconnect each code to a person, so the coded records are not anonymous just because names are absent. Restricting the key helps limit access but does not erase the link. Photographs can identify people even if the name field is removed.

W3 Share the result the audience needs

The fictional class has drawing 8, music 7 and coding 5. Write the result needed to choose a workshop. A pupil suggests posting every vote slip online as proof. Explain a lower-exposure way to check and share the result. Why can a total about a one-person club still reveal a choice?

Drawing leads with 8; totals are 8, 7 and 5, adding to 20. The teacher checks the slips and totals, then shares the three totals with the class rather than public individual slips. A one-person club total can be linked to its only member, revealing that person’s choice.

W4 Design a different minimal-data task

A fictional library wants only the total number of books needing repair in each category: cover, page or binding. It does not need to contact borrowers. A draft repair sheet asks for borrower name, home address, book repair category and borrower photograph. Design a plan: state the purpose, required and removed fields, who handles source sheets, what totals are shared with the librarian, and who disposes of sheets at what agreed point. Use no real borrower details.

Purpose: count repair needs by category. Keep repair category; remove borrower name, address and photograph because no borrower contact is required. An assigned teacher or librarian handles the source sheets, checks counts and shares category totals with the librarian. Name that adult as responsible for disposal after totals are verified and the counting task closes, following the agreed school process. Other justified restricted roles and clear disposal triggers are acceptable.

W5 Respond to an unnecessary upload

Someone plans to upload the library repair sheet with borrower details to an unapproved AI tool to add three totals. Explain a smaller-input alternative and a no-upload method. If the sheet was already shared, state a suitable first response and one claim you must not make about deleting it.

Use only fictional category counts in this lesson; for real work check teacher-approved tools and data rules before any upload. Add totals on paper or with an approved local calculator. If shared, stop further sharing and tell the teacher or trusted adult promptly so the school process can be followed. Do not claim that deleting the visible message removes all copies or backups.
