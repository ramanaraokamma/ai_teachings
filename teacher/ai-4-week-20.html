Explain the mechanism

Explain the mechanism

Trace the information, fitted behavior, and evaluation proof separately.

QUESTION Choose and justify a baseline under an extreme value.

MODEL IDEA Mean and median are transparent regression baselines; outliers affect them differently.

PROOF NEEDED Use an unseen case, visible calculation, error analysis, and a stated limitation.

UNDER THE HOOD Heights are 10, 11, 12, 13, and 54 cm. Mean = 100 ÷ 5 = 20 cm.

Draw a fresh data → model → prediction → evaluation trace:

Predict first. Then check each step. A correct answer without visible evidence cannot be audited.

STEP 1 Heights are 10, 11, 12, 13, and 54 cm.

STEP 2 Mean = 100 ÷ 5 = 20 cm.

STEP 3 Median is 12 cm, the middle ordered value.

STEP 4 For typical-height absolute error, median is more robust; still report why 54 is present.

CHECK Verify the denominator, unit, split, class definition, and data version before interpreting a score.

Recalculate, retrace, or restate the evidence independently:

Concrete teaching case

Training heights are 2,4,12 cm. Compare constant mean and median baselines.

Compute both and training absolute errors.

Exact explanation to model

Mean=6, median=4. Mean errors 4,2,6 total12; median errors 2,0,8 total10. Median minimises absolute error here.

Explain the added worked example

Before a complex regression model, fit a simple constant predictor. A mean baseline uses the training average. A median baseline uses the middle training target. They ignore features but provide a comparison that is easy to reproduce. Evaluation targets must not determine the constants.

Use fictional classroom data throughout this investigation. Keep the task, units, reference labels and data split visible as you reason. Earlier weeks supplied the model-building process; this week examines constant prediction baselines.

Training targets [2, 4, 6, 12] have mean 24/4=6 and median (4+6)/2=5. On separate targets [4, 8], the mean baseline predicts [6, 6] with absolute errors [2, 2], averaging 2. The median baseline predicts [5, 5] with errors [1, 3], also averaging 2.

Trace the calculation on paper before running this optional Python check. Use the stated fictional inputs and compare intermediate values. The code checks this example; your independent workbook case uses different evidence.

Why this result follows

Read the picture in order. Mean MAE=(4+2)/2=3 cm; median MAE=(1+5)/2=3 cm. The arrows show which recorded input or intermediate result each decision uses. Explain the deciding step aloud before checking the changed case; pointing to the final answer alone does not show how it follows.

What this example does not establish

Mean and median have different loss properties, but their held-out performance depends on the particular evaluation cases.

Explanations for the additional workbook examples

W1 Fit two constants

Find the mean and median of the worked training targets.

Teacher explanation: Mean 6; median 5.

For the mean baseline, sum the worked training targets and divide by their count to obtain 6. For the median baseline, sort the training targets and take the middle value, 5. Freeze these choices before examining evaluation targets; choosing a new constant from evaluation outcomes would contaminate the comparison.

W2 Score separately

Calculate MAE for both baselines on [4, 8].

Teacher explanation: Both score 2; their per-record absolute errors differ.

For constant 6, absolute errors on targets 4 and 8 are 2 and 2, so MAE is (2+2)/2 = 2. For constant 5, the errors are 1 and 3, so MAE is (1+3)/2 = 2. Equal averages can hide different per-record errors; retain the error table as well as the summary.

W3 Inspect an extreme value

Replace 12 by 40. What changes?

Teacher explanation: Mean becomes 13; median stays 5. Investigate validity before deciding treatment.

Training [1, 3, 5, 15], evaluation [3, 9]. Fit both constants and calculate MAE.

Teacher explanation: Mean 6 and median 4. Mean errors [3, 3] MAE 3; median errors [1, 5] MAE 3.

W5 Prevent leakage

Why not predict the evaluation mean as the baseline?

Teacher explanation: It fits using evaluation answers and makes the baseline unavailable for genuinely unseen cases.
