# Unified curriculum coverage review

The current seven-level pathway is the only main student and teacher curriculum. The comparison maps every one of the 144 earlier weeks to its relevant latest lesson, retains all original lessons, workbook practice, teaching explanations, illustrations, topic-specific visual panels and fresh transfer challenges as optional topic practice, and keeps protected original downloads intact. This is topic continuity, not a claim of identical week-by-week pacing or improved classroom outcomes.

## Findings and changes

- Foundations mostly continue at the same weeks. The earlier fairness lesson includes broader questions about error costs and access; it is retained beside the latest performance-comparison lesson rather than discarded.
- Algorithms and data are expanded into executable Python, validation, units, schemas, debugging and reproducible investigations. Earlier classifier/tree/overfitting topics are spread between foundation and machine-learning levels.
- Machine learning moves from the earlier Level 4 to current Level 3. Calibration, precision/recall, leakage, subgroup evaluation and model cards have explicit current lessons.
- Generative systems move from the earlier Level 3 to current Level 5. Prompting, image composition, audio, grounding, copyright and evaluation practice are retained at the matching latest lessons.
- Neural networks, service engineering and research add three substantial areas to the pathway.
- Grade placement changes: several topics that appeared at Grades 7–8 now receive their full treatment at Grades 8–10. This is a deliberate sequencing change, not equivalent grade-specific coverage. Teachers can use retained practice as an introduction after checking prerequisites.
- The latest reader had lost editable answer fields; these are restored with print support. It also had fewer alternate visual cases and transfer tasks; all 144 retained practice chapters are now discoverable from their mapped latest lessons.
- A single seven-level overview replaces the two-edition dashboard. Old week URLs resolve by this topic mapping, never by assuming equal week numbers. Practice chapters lead back to the matched current lesson rather than an earlier course map.
- Current Word/PDF downloads preserve approved bytes. New online/study-edition aids and retained practice remain separate resources, clearly labeled; they are not silently represented as present in unchanged Word/PDF files.

## Method and limits

The comparison uses all earlier week titles, source lesson blocks, learning targets, workbook structure, review transfer tasks and topic-plan visual activities, then records a hand-selected destination for every topic. The JSON stores source hashes and examples of learning evidence. Retaining the full practice chapters prevents unique activities or resources from disappearing even where the current lesson uses a different case. This is an editorial coverage audit; it does not establish that every old activity is a required latest learning outcome or that every student understands the new sequence.
